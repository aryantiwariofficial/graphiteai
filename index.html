The AI Act timeline

What already applies, and what comes next

The Act phases in over four years. The Digital Omnibus on AI, in force since 27 July 2026, moved the high-risk deadlines back, but the earlier obligations still apply.

  1. 1 Aug 2024

    Act enters into force

    Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 is published and the phase-in clock starts.

    In effect
  2. 2 Feb 2025

    Prohibited practices banned

    Article 5 bans and AI literacy duties for staff apply.

    In effect
  3. 2 Aug 2025

    General-purpose AI rules

    GPAI model obligations, governance and penalties apply.

    In effect
  4. 27 Jul 2026

    Digital Omnibus in force

    Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 delays the high-risk deadlines.

    In effect
  5. 2 Aug 2026

    Transparency duties

    Article 50 disclosure rules for chatbots, deepfakes and AI content apply.

    In effect
  6. 2 Dec 2026

    New bans and watermarking

    Two new Article 5 bans apply, and the marking grace period for existing systems ends.

    Next
  7. 2 Dec 2027

    Stand-alone high-risk AI

    Annex III systems, such as hiring, credit and education, must comply.

    Upcoming
  8. 2 Aug 2028

    High-risk AI in products

    Annex I systems built into regulated products, such as medical devices, must comply.

    Upcoming

What it checks

Data, code and documents, read against the law

The agent maps each part of your system to the articles that apply to it, and cites the article behind every finding.

Data

  • Art. 10Origin, preparation steps, bias examination and gaps in training, validation and test sets
  • Art. 10(3)Whether data is relevant and representative for the intended use
  • Art. 5Flags prohibited sources, such as untargeted scraping of facial images

Code

  • Art. 12Automatic logging and record-keeping across the system's lifetime
  • Art. 14Human oversight: the ability to monitor, override and stop
  • Art. 15Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity measures
  • Art. 50Disclosure when people interact with AI or see AI-generated content

Documents

  • Art. 9Risk management system across the lifecycle
  • Art. 11Technical documentation against Annex IV
  • Art. 13Instructions for use for deployers
  • Art. 53For GPAI models: training-data summary and copyright policy

How it works

Connect, scan, prove

01

Connect your sources

Give the agent read access to your repositories, data platforms and document drives: GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, Amazon S3, Google Drive, SharePoint, Confluence and Notion.

02

The agent scans

It classifies your system's risk tier, works out which obligations apply, and checks each source against them, citing the article for every finding.

Assign gaps, apply suggested fixes, and keep the dashboard and report current. The agent re-checks on every change.

Questions

Does Graphite make us compliant?

No tool can do that alone. The agent finds gaps, drafts documentation and keeps the evidence current; your legal and risk teams still decide and sign off.

Will the agent change our code or data?

Only with your approval. It proposes fixes and drafts, and you choose what to apply.

Is my system high-risk?

Systems used in areas such as hiring, credit, education, essential services and critical infrastructure usually are. The agent proposes a classification with its reasoning for you and your advisers to confirm.

Where is our data processed?

In the EU. By default, scans run in EU-hosted cloud regions and your data never leaves the EU; Enterprise customers can also run the agent inside their own cloud. Source content is read only during a scan and is never used to train models. Findings and evidence are kept for as long as your subscription is active, and you can delete them at any time.
